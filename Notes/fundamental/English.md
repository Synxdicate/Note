**English** — covering both language learning and mastery:

## 1. Phonetics & Pronunciation

- **Phonemic Alphabet (IPA)** — 44 sounds in English
- **Vowel sounds** — short, long, diphthongs
- **Consonant sounds** — voiced vs unvoiced
- **Stress & rhythm** — word stress, sentence stress
- **Intonation** — rising, falling, rise-fall patterns
- **Connected speech** — linking, elision, assimilation
- **Minimal pairs** — ship/sheep, bit/beat, cat/cut
- **Silent letters** — knife, knight, psychology
- **Syllables** — counting, stress placement

---

## 2. Vocabulary

- **Core vocabulary** — 2,000 most common words (covers 90% of speech)
- **Academic Word List (AWL)** — 570 word families for formal writing
- **Word families** — run, runner, running, ran
- **Collocations** — words that go together naturally (make a decision, do homework)
- **Idioms** — under the weather, break a leg, piece of cake
- **Phrasal verbs** — give up, look into, turn down, carry on
- **Synonyms & antonyms** — expand range, avoid repetition
- **Context clues** — guessing meaning from context
- **Register** — formal vs informal vocabulary
- **False friends** — words that look similar but differ in meaning

## 3. Grammar

### Parts of Speech

- **Nouns** — common, proper, abstract, collective, countable/uncountable
- **Pronouns** — personal, possessive, reflexive, relative
- **Verbs** — action, linking, auxiliary, modal
- **Adjectives** — comparative, superlative, order of adjectives
- **Adverbs** — manner, frequency, degree, time, place
- **Prepositions** — in, on, at, by, with, through, between
- **Conjunctions** — coordinating (FANBOYS), subordinating, correlative
- **Articles** — a, an, the (definite vs indefinite)
- **Interjections** — oh!, wow!, oops!

### Tenses (12 Core Tenses)

|Tense|Example|
|---|---|
|Simple Present|I eat rice|
|Present Continuous|I am eating|
|Present Perfect|I have eaten|
|Present Perfect Continuous|I have been eating|
|Simple Past|I ate|
|Past Continuous|I was eating|
|Past Perfect|I had eaten|
|Past Perfect Continuous|I had been eating|
|Simple Future|I will eat|
|Future Continuous|I will be eating|
|Future Perfect|I will have eaten|
|Future Perfect Continuous|I will have been eating|

### Sentence Structure

- **Subject + Verb + Object** (SVO) — basic English order
- Simple, compound, complex, compound-complex sentences
- Clauses — independent vs dependent
- Phrases — noun, verb, adjective, adverbial, prepositional

### Key Grammar Topics

- **Subject-verb agreement** — The team is/are playing
- **Articles** — a dog, an apple, the sun
- **Modal verbs** — can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would
- **Passive voice** — The book was written by him
- **Reported speech** — She said that she was tired
- **Conditionals** — zero, first, second, third, mixed
- **Relative clauses** — who, which, that, whose, where
- **Gerunds & infinitives** — I enjoy swimming / I want to swim
- **Quantifiers** — some, any, much, many, few, little
- **Determiners** — this, that, these, those, each, every

## 4. The Four Core Skills

### Reading

- Skimming — get general idea quickly
- Scanning — find specific information
- Intensive reading — detailed understanding
- Extensive reading — reading for pleasure/fluency
- Inference — reading between the lines
- Text structure — introduction, body, conclusion
- Identifying main idea vs supporting details
- Understanding tone, purpose, audience

### Writing

- **Sentence level** — clear, varied, grammatically correct
- **Paragraph structure** — topic sentence, supporting ideas, conclusion
- **Essay structure** — introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion
- **Cohesion & coherence** — logical flow, transitions
- **Linking words** — however, therefore, furthermore, in contrast
- **Punctuation** — period, comma, semicolon, colon, apostrophe, dash
- **Writing types:**
    - Narrative — storytelling
    - Descriptive — vivid description
    - Expository — explaining/informing
    - Persuasive/Argumentative — convincing reader
    - Analytical — breaking down ideas
    - Academic — formal, structured, referenced

### Listening

- Identifying main idea vs detail
- Understanding different accents (British, American, Australian)
- Recognizing fast/natural speech features
- Note-taking strategies
- Understanding implied meaning
- Following instructions and lectures
- Listening for attitude and opinion

### Speaking

- Fluency vs accuracy balance
- Pronunciation clarity
- Turn-taking in conversation
- Discourse markers — well, you know, actually, I mean
- Functional language — agreeing, disagreeing, asking for clarification
- Formal vs informal speaking styles
- Presentations and public speaking
- Small talk and social language

## 5. Spelling & Punctuation

### Spelling Rules

- i before e except after c (receive, believe)
- Silent e dropping — make → making
- Doubling consonants — run → running
- Y to i changes — happy → happier
- Common irregular spellings — necessary, beautiful, occurrence

### Punctuation

- **Period (.)** — ends sentences
- **Comma (,)** — lists, clauses, pauses
- **Apostrophe (')** — possession, contractions
- **Semicolon (;)** — joins related independent clauses
- **Colon (:)** — introduces list or explanation
- **Quotation marks (" ")** — direct speech, titles
- **Dash (— or -)** — emphasis, interruption
- **Hyphen (-)** — compound words
- **Ellipsis (...)** — trailing off, omission
- **Exclamation mark (!)** — strong emotion
- **Question mark (?)** — direct questions

## 10. Language Learning Strategies

- **Spaced repetition** — Anki flashcards for vocabulary
- **Immersion** — movies, podcasts, books in English
- **Output practice** — speaking and writing daily
- **Error correction** — learn from mistakes
- **Language exchange** — practice with native speakers
- **Journaling** — write daily in English
- **Shadowing** — mimic native speaker rhythm and pronunciation
- **Extensive reading** — read books at comfortable level
